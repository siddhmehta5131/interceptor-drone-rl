**Constant-g (Step) Maneuver**
The target applies a sudden, constant lateral acceleration at a specific time to exploit the physical reaction lag of the missile.


$$a_T(t) = n_T g \cdot u(t - t_0)$$

**Sinusoidal (Weave) Maneuver**
The target continuously oscillates to shift the predicted intercept point, attempting to match the resonant frequency that maximizes the missile's steady-state miss distance.


$$a_T(t) = a_{T,\max} \sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T)$$

**Terminal Break Turn**
The target flies straight until the time-to-go ($t_{go}$) falls below the missile's aerodynamic response time, then instantly pulls maximum Gs perpendicular to the Line of Sight (LOS).


$$a_T(t) = a_{n,\max} \quad \text{when} \quad t_{go} < t_{\text{break}}$$

**Energy Depletion Maneuver**
The target steers directly away from the missile to minimize closing velocity ($v_{cl}$), extending the flight time to force the missile to coast and bleed off speed.


$$v_{cl} = v_M \cos\delta - v_T \cos\theta \xrightarrow{\text{minimize}} v_M \cos\delta - v_T$$

**Split-S / Vertical Maneuver**
The target rolls inverted ($\phi = 180^\circ$) and pulls maximum Gs downward, utilizing gravity to minimize turn radius while trading altitude for kinetic energy.


$$\dot{\gamma} = -\frac{n_T g + g \cos\gamma}{v_T}$$

**Square (Bang-Bang) Maneuver**
The target instantly snaps between its maximum positive and negative structural G-limits to create a discontinuous acceleration profile that maximizes the error integral.


$$a_T(t) = a_{T,\max} \cdot \text{sgn}(\sin(\omega_T t))$$

**Scissor Maneuver**
The target weaves across the pursuit axis while applying maximum longitudinal braking ($a_t < 0$) to force the faster missile to overshoot its position.


$$v_x = v_T \cos(\Delta\psi), \quad a_t = a_{t,\min}$$

**Notching (Doppler Defeat)**
The target steers exactly perpendicular to the radar's LOS to drop its relative radial velocity to zero, vanishing into the radar's ground-clutter filters.


$$v_r = v_T \cos\theta = 0 \quad \text{when} \quad \theta = 90^\circ$$

**Turning into the Missile**
The target turns head-on to maximize closing speed, which artificially spikes the LOS angular rate ($\dot{\lambda}$) beyond the missile seeker's mechanical tracking limits.


$$v_{cl} = v_M \cos\delta + v_T \vert{}\cos\theta\vert{} \quad \text{when} \quad \theta > 90^\circ$$

**Crank Maneuver**
The target flies at the absolute edge of its own radar's gimbal limit ($\theta_{\text{gimbal}}$) to maintain a sensor track while minimizing its forward closure rate toward the threat.


$$v_{r,\text{target}} = v_T \cos(\theta_{\text{gimbal}})$$

**Speed Change Maneuver**
The target accelerates or decelerates linearly without turning, injecting a false tangential acceleration component perpendicular to the LOS.


$$a_{Tt}(t) = a_{t,T}(t)\sin\theta$$

**Minimum Range (Close-In) Turn**
A slower target leverages the kinematic property that turn radius scales with the square of velocity, physically out-turning a faster missile inside its minimum turning circle.


$$R_T = \frac{v_T^2}{a_T} \ll R_M = \frac{v_M^2}{a_M}$$

**Dive into Dense Air**
The target dives to a lower altitude $h$ where exponential increases in atmospheric density $\rho$ maximize aerodynamic drag on the unpowered missile.


$$\rho(h) = \rho_0 e^{-h/H_s}$$

**Spiral (Corkscrew) Maneuver**
The target flies a descending 3D spiral while continuously increasing its rotation frequency ($\omega_s$) to guarantee it sweeps through the missile's weakest resonant tracking frequency.


$$\omega_s(t) = \omega_0 + \alpha t$$


Each of the 14 evasive maneuvers is defined below using a unified 3D and planar engagement framework.Baseline Engagement & Kinematic FrameworkThe relative geometry between a missile $M$ at position $\mathbf{r}_M$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}_M$ and a target $T$ at position $\mathbf{r}_T$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}_T$ is governed by the range vector $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_T - \mathbf{r}_M$, scalar range $r = \Vert{}\mathbf{r}\Vert{}$, and closing velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_{cl} = -\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{v}_M - \mathbf{v}_T$. The relative equation of motion is $\ddot{\mathbf{r}}(t) = \mathbf{a}_T(t) - \mathbf{a}_M(t)$, where $\mathbf{a}_T$ and $\mathbf{a}_M$ are the target and missile acceleration vectors.   In a planar engagement where $\theta$ is the target's angle with respect to the Line-of-Sight (LOS) and $\delta$ is the missile's lead angle with respect to the LOS, the closing velocity $v_{cl} = -\dot{r}$ and LOS angular rate $\dot{\lambda}$ are:$$v_{cl} = -\dot{r} = v_M \cos\delta - v_T \cos\theta$$   $$\dot{\lambda} = \frac{v_T \sin\theta - v_M \sin\delta}{r}$$The exact nonlinear second derivative of the LOS angle $\lambda(t)$ is:$$\ddot{\lambda}(t) + \frac{2\dot{r}(t)}{r(t)}\dot{\lambda}(t) + \frac{\ddot{r}(t)}{r(t)}\lambda(t) = \frac{a_{T,\perp}(t) - a_{M,\perp}(t)}{r(t)}$$   A Proportional Navigation (PN) interceptor commands lateral acceleration $a_c(t)$ perpendicular to the LOS proportional to the effective navigation ratio $N$, closing velocity $v_{cl}$, and LOS rate $\dot{\lambda}$:$$a_c(t) = N v_{cl} \dot{\lambda}(t) = N \frac{\text{ZEM}}{t_{go}^2}$$
where $t_{go} = t_F - t$ is the time-to-go until intercept time $t_F$, and $\text{ZEM} = y(t) + \dot{y}(t)t_{go}$ is the zero-effort miss distance.   1. Constant-g Maneuver (Step Maneuver)Control Law:
The target applies a constant normal acceleration of magnitude $n_T g$ (where $n_T$ is the load factor in Gs and $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$) starting at $t = t_0$. In a horizontal plane (fixed altitude, bank angle $\phi_0 = \cos^{-1}(1/n_T)$), the lateral acceleration is a step input:
   $$a_T(t) = n_T g \cdot u(t - t_0)$$
where $u(t - t_0)$ is the Heaviside unit step function.   Kinematic Trajectory:At constant speed $v_T$, the target executes a circular arc with constant angular turn rate $\omega_T$ and turn radius $R_T$:$$\omega_T = \frac{n_T g}{v_T}, \quad R_T = \frac{v_T^2}{n_T g}$$Integrating from initial heading $\psi_0$ and position $(x_0, y_0)$ at $t_0 = 0$:$$\psi(t) = \psi_0 + \omega_T t$$$$x_T(t) = x_0 + R_T\big[\sin(\psi_0 + \omega_T t) - \sin\psi_0\big]$$$$y_T(t) = y_0 - R_T\big[\cos(\psi_0 + \omega_T t) - \cos\psi_0\big]$$Engagement & Miss Distance Mathematics:A constant target maneuver $a_T$ adds a quadratic term to the zero-effort miss:$$\text{ZEM} = y(t) + \dot{y}(t)t_{go} + 0.5 a_T t_{go}^2$$
For an idealized inertialess PN missile, the lateral separation $y(t)$ is:
   $$y(t) = C(t_F - t)^N + \left(\frac{t_F - t}{N - 1} - \frac{(t_F - t)^2}{(N - 1)(N - 2)}\right)a_T$$
When the interceptor has a first-order flight control lag $\tau_1$, the Laplace transform of the adjoint miss step response is $Z_4(s) = s^{-3}\left(\frac{s}{s + 1/\tau_1}\right)^N$. For $N = 4$, the exact analytical miss distance caused by a step maneuver of magnitude $a_T$ is:
   $$y(t_F) = a_T t_F^2 e^{-t_F/\tau_1}\left(0.5 - \frac{t_F}{6\tau_1}\right)$$   2. Sinusoidal or Barrel Roll Maneuver2D Planar Sinusoidal Weave:The target applies a periodic lateral acceleration with amplitude $a_{T,\max} = n_T g$, weaving frequency $\omega_T$, and starting phase $\phi_T$:$$a_T(t) = n_T g \sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T)$$
Integrating twice yields the lateral velocity and lateral displacement:
   $$v_{y,T}(t) = v_{y0} - \frac{n_T g}{\omega_T}\cos(\omega_T t + \phi_T), \quad y_T(t) = y_0 + v_{y0}t - \frac{n_T g}{\omega_T^2}\sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T)$$3D Helical Barrel Roll:In a 3D barrel roll along a nominal flight axis (the $x$-axis), the target maintains a constant normal acceleration magnitude $n_T g$ while rotating its bank angle at a constant roll rate $p = \omega_T$:$$\mathbf{a}_T(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ n_T g \cos(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \\ n_T g \sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \end{bmatrix}$$Integrating twice gives the 3D velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_T(t)$ and 3D helical position vector $\mathbf{r}_T(t)$:$$\mathbf{v}_T(t) = \begin{bmatrix} v_{x0} \\ \frac{n_T g}{\omega_T}\sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \\ -\frac{n_T g}{\omega_T}\cos(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{r}_T(t) = \begin{bmatrix} x_0 + v_{x0}t \\ y_0 - R_b \cos(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \\ z_0 - R_b \sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T) \end{bmatrix}$$where $R_b = \frac{n_T g}{\omega_T^2}$ is the radius of the barrel roll cylinder, and $\gamma_h = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{n_T g}{\omega_T v_{x0}}\right)$ is the helical pitch angle.Engagement & Optimal Weaving Frequency Mathematics:In the frequency domain, the steady-state miss distance against a weaving target is:$$y(t_F) = \vert{}P(t_F, i\omega_T)\vert{}\sin\big(\omega_T t_F + \varphi(t_F, i\omega_T)\big)$$
For a first-order missile guidance system with time constant $\tau_1$ against a maneuver amplitude $n_T g$, the amplitude and phase characteristics are:
   $$\vert{}P(t_F, i\omega_T)\vert{} = n_T g \omega_T^{N-2}\left(\omega_T^2 + \frac{1}{\tau_1^2}\right)^{-N/2}$$   $$\varphi(t_F, i\omega_T) = -\pi + N\frac{\pi}{2} - N\tan^{-1}(\omega_T\tau_1)$$
For $N = 3$ and $1g$ amplitude, this simplifies to $y(t_F) = \frac{g\omega_T}{(\omega_T^2 + 1/\tau_1^2)^{1.5}}\sin(\omega_T t_F + \pi/2 - 3\tan^{-1}(\omega_T\tau_1))$. Differentiating $\vert{}P(t_F, i\omega_T)\vert{}$ with respect to $\omega_T$ and setting the derivative to zero yields the exact optimal weaving frequency $\omega_{T,\text{opt}}$ that maximizes miss distance:
   $$\frac{d}{d\omega_T}\left[\omega_T^{N-2}\left(\omega_T^2 + \frac{1}{\tau_1^2}\right)^{-N/2}\right] = 0 \implies \omega_{T,\text{opt}} = \frac{1}{\tau_1}\sqrt{\frac{N - 2}{2}}$$3. Terminal Break TurnControl Law:The target waits until the time-to-go $t_{go} = r / v_{cl}$ drops to a critical threshold $t_{\text{break}}$ inside the interceptor's autopilot response lag, then applies maximum normal acceleration $a_{n,\max}$ strictly perpendicular to the instantaneous 3D LOS vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$:$$\mathbf{a}_T(t) = \begin{cases} \mathbf{0}, & t_{go} > t_{\text{break}} \\ a_{n,\max} \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\perp}, & 0 \le t_{go} \le t_{\text{break}} \end{cases}$$where the unit break direction vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\perp}$ lies in the plane of $\mathbf{v}_T$ and $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{v}_T$:$$\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\perp} = \frac{(\mathbf{v}_T \times \boldsymbol{\lambda}) \times \mathbf{v}_T}{\Vert{}(\mathbf{v}_T \times \boldsymbol{\lambda}) \times \mathbf{v}_T\Vert{}}$$Optimal Break Timing ($t_{\text{break}}$):To maximize the terminal miss $y(t_F)$, $t_{\text{break}}$ must coincide with the global maximum of the interceptor's step miss response $y(t_{go})$.For a first-order missile lag $\tau_1$ with $N = 3$, the step miss response is $y(t_{go}) = \frac{1}{2}a_{n,\max} t_{go}^2 e^{-t_{go}/\tau_1}$. Differentiating with respect to $t_{go}$ yields the exact optimal break time:$$\frac{d}{dt_{go}}\left(\frac{1}{2}a_{n,\max} t_{go}^2 e^{-t_{go}/\tau_1}\right) = 0 \implies t_{\text{break}} = 2\tau_1$$
For $N = 4$, differentiating $y(t_{go}) = a_{n,\max} t_{go}^2 e^{-t_{go}/\tau_1}\left(0.5 - \frac{t_{go}}{6\tau_1}\right)$ yields:
   $$\frac{d}{dt_{go}}\left[t_{go}^2 e^{-t_{go}/\tau_1}\left(0.5 - \frac{t_{go}}{6\tau_1}\right)\right] = 0 \implies t_{\text{break}} = (3 - \sqrt{3})\tau_1 \approx 1.268\tau_1$$
4. Energy Depletion ManeuverControl Law:The target steers its unit velocity vector $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T = \mathbf{v}_T / v_T$ away from the missile to align with the LOS vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\mathbf{r}_T - \mathbf{r}_M)/r$ (driving $\theta \to 0^\circ$) while applying maximum forward thrust acceleration $a_{t,\max}$ and superimposing a lateral weave to induce drag on the pursuer:$$\mathbf{a}_T(t) = a_{t,\max}\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T + \text{sat}_{a_{n,\max}}\left(K_p \frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda} - (\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T)\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T}{\Vert{}\boldsymbol{\lambda} - (\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T)\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T\Vert{}} + A_w \sin(\omega_w t)\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T\right)$$Engagement & Energy Depletion Mathematics:
As $\theta \to 0^\circ$, $\cos\theta \to 1$, which minimizes the closing velocity:
   $$v_{cl} = v_M \cos\delta - v_T \cos\theta \xrightarrow{\theta \to 0^\circ} v_M \cos\delta - v_T$$
This stretches the required intercept time $t_F$ and forces the missile to traverse a total path length $S_M \approx r_0 \frac{v_M}{v_M - v_T}$. During its coast/glide phase (thrust $T_M = 0$), the missile's speed $v_M(t)$ decays according to the drag equation (comprising zero-lift parasite drag $C_{D0}$ and maneuver-induced drag $k_{\text{ind}} a_M^2$):
   $$\frac{dv_M}{dt} = -\frac{Q_M S_M C_{D0}}{m_M} - \frac{k_{\text{ind}} m_M}{Q_M S_M}a_M^2(t) - g\sin\gamma_M$$
where $Q_M = \frac{1}{2}\rho v_M^2$ is the dynamic pressure and $a_M(t) \approx N v_{cl}\dot{\lambda}$ is the missile's lateral acceleration induced by the target's weave. Kinematic defeat is achieved when the missile's speed falls below the target's speed ($v_M(t) \le v_T \implies v_{cl} \le 0$) while $r(t) > 0$.   5. Split-S / Vertical ManeuverControl Law & Two-Phase Kinematics:The Split-S is a vertical-plane escape maneuver consisting of an instantaneous half-roll ($\Delta\phi = \pi$) followed by a maximum-G downward half-loop ($\Delta\gamma = -\pi$) that trades altitude for speed and reverses heading by $180^\circ$.Phase 1 — Roll Inversion ($0 \le t < t_{\text{roll}}$):The target commands roll angular acceleration $\dot{p}$ and roll rate $p$ to rotate bank angle $\phi$ from $0$ to $\pi\text{ rad}$ while holding normal pitch acceleration at zero:$$\dot{\phi} = p(t), \quad \phi(t_{\text{roll}}) = \pi, \quad \gamma(t_{\text{roll}}) = 0$$Phase 2 — Vertical Pull-Through ($t_{\text{roll}} \le t \le t_{\text{exit}}$):With $\phi = \pi$, the body $+z$-axis points upward in the NED frame. Pulling maximum body normal load factor $n_T g$ accelerates the target downward with gravity assisting the curvature:$$\frac{dv_T}{dt} = a_{t,T} + g\sin\vert{}\gamma\vert{}$$$$\frac{d\gamma}{dt} = -\frac{n_T g + g\cos\gamma}{v_T(t)}$$$$\frac{dx_T}{dt} = v_T(t)\cos\gamma(t), \quad \frac{dz_T}{dt} = -v_T(t)\sin\gamma(t) \quad (\text{with }+z\text{ downward})$$The maneuver completes at $t_{\text{exit}}$ when $\gamma(t_{\text{exit}}) = -\pi$, placing the target in level flight on the opposite heading $\psi_{\text{exit}} = \psi_0 + \pi$.Minimum Altitude Loss ($\Delta z_{\text{Split-S}}$):Dividing $\frac{dz_T}{dt}$ by $\frac{d\gamma}{dt}$ and integrating from $\gamma = 0$ to $\gamma = -\pi$ gives the exact vertical drop required to execute the Split-S:$$\Delta z_{\text{Split-S}} = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{v_T^2(\theta)\sin\theta}{n_T g + g\cos\theta}\,d\theta$$Assuming constant speed $\bar{v}_T$, this integral evaluates in closed form to:$$\Delta z_{\text{Split-S}} = \frac{\bar{v}_T^2}{g}\ln\left(\frac{n_T + 1}{n_T - 1}\right)$$6. Square or Bang-Bang ManeuverControl Law:Instead of varying acceleration sinusoidally, the target switches its normal acceleration discontinuously between its structural saturation limits $+a_{T,\max}$ and $-a_{T,\max}$:$$a_T(t) = a_{T,\max}\cdot\text{sgn}\big(\sin(\omega_T t + \phi_T)\big)$$
In a realistic airframe with roll or actuator lag $\tau_T$, the achieved acceleration is governed by $\tau_T \dot{a}_{T,\text{actual}} + a_{T,\text{actual}} = a_T(t)$.   Optimal Control / Game-Theoretic Switching Law:
From terminal miss integral analysis, $y(t_F) = \int_0^{t_F} P(t_F, \tau)a_T(\tau)\,d\tau$, where $P(t_F, \tau)$ is the impulse response of the missile guidance system. By Pontryagin's Maximum Principle (and Hölder's inequality), the target maximizes the absolute miss distance $\vert{}y(t_F)\vert{}$ by switching acceleration polarity whenever $P(t_F, t_{go})$ changes sign:
   $$a_T^*(t_{go}) = a_{T,\max}\cdot\text{sgn}\big(P(t_F, t_{go})\big) \implies \vert{}y(t_F)\vert{}_{\max} = a_{T,\max}\int_0^{t_F} \vert{}P(t_F, \tau)\vert{}\,d\tau$$Fourier Series & Miss Amplification:Expanding the periodic square wave in a Fourier series yields:$$a_T(t) = \frac{4 a_{T,\max}}{\pi}\sum_{k=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}\sin\big(k(\omega_T t + \phi_T)\big)$$
Because the missile guidance transfer function $\vert{}P(t_F, i\omega)\vert{}$ acts as a bandpass filter peaked at $\omega_{T,\text{opt}}$ and attenuates higher harmonics ($k \ge 3$), the fundamental harmonic ($k=1$) dominates the steady-state miss distance:
   $$y_{\text{square}}(t_F) \approx \frac{4}{\pi}\vert{}P(t_F, i\omega_T)\vert{}\sin\big(\omega_T t_F + \varphi(t_F, i\omega_T)\big) \approx 1.273\,y_{\text{sine}}(t_F)$$A bang-bang square maneuver therefore produces a 27.3% larger steady-state miss distance than a sinusoidal maneuver of the same peak G-limit.7. Scissor Maneuver (Flat & Rolling Scissors)Flat Scissors Control Law:The target executes periodic bank-angle reversals across a reference line of flight while applying maximum longitudinal braking ($a_{t,T} = a_{t,\min} < 0$) to force an overtaking pursuer to overshoot in down-range coordinate $x$:$$a_{t,T}(t) = a_{t,\min} < 0, \quad \phi_{\text{cmd}}(t) = \phi_{\max}\cdot\text{sgn}\big(\psi_{\max} - \vert{}\psi_T(t) - \psi_{\text{ref}}\vert{}\big)$$where $\psi_{\max} \in [\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ is the scissor crossing angle limit.Kinematic Trajectory & Forward Velocity Reduction:$$\dot{v}_T(t) = a_{t,\min}, \quad \dot{\psi}_T(t) = \frac{g\tan\phi(t)}{v_T(t)}$$$$\dot{x}_T(t) = v_T(t)\cos\big(\psi_T(t) - \psi_{\text{ref}}\big), \quad \dot{y}_T(t) = v_T(t)\sin\big(\psi_T(t) - \psi_{\text{ref}}\big)$$Over a single scissor half-cycle where $\Delta\psi = \psi_T - \psi_{\text{ref}}$ sweeps from $-\psi_{\max}$ to $+\psi_{\max}$ at constant turn rate, the target's mean forward velocity along the reference axis drops to:$$\bar{v}_{x,T} = \frac{1}{2\psi_{\max}}\int_{-\psi_{\max}}^{\psi_{\max}} v_T \cos(\Delta\psi)\,d(\Delta\psi) = v_T \frac{\sin\psi_{\max}}{\psi_{\max}}$$For $\psi_{\max} = \frac{\pi}{2}$ ($90^\circ$ weaves), $\bar{v}_{x,T} = \frac{2}{\pi}v_T \approx 0.637 v_T$. An overshoot occurs ($x_M(t) > x_T(t)$ and $v_{cl} < 0$) at time $t_{\text{overshoot}}$ satisfying:$$\int_0^{t_{\text{overshoot}}} \big[v_M(\tau)\cos\Delta\psi_M(\tau) - v_T(\tau)\cos\Delta\psi_T(\tau)\big]\,d\tau = x_T(0) - x_M(0)$$Rolling Scissors (3D):Instead of reversing $\text{sgn}(\phi)$, the target commands a continuous roll rate $p(t) = \omega_s$ coupled with pitch rate $q = n_T g / v_T$ and negative tangential acceleration $a_{t,\min} < 0$, tracing a decelerating helix whose radius shrinks over time as $R(t) = \frac{(v_{T0} + a_{t,\min}t)^2}{n_T g}$.
8. Notching (Beaming / Doppler Clutter Notch)Radar Doppler & Kinematic Geometry:A pulse-Doppler radar separates moving targets from ground clutter by measuring the radial velocity component along the LOS vector $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$. The radial velocity of the target with respect to a stationary ground reference is $v_{r,T} = \mathbf{v}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda} = v_T \cos\theta$, producing a Doppler shift relative to mainlobe ground clutter of:$$\Delta f_D = \frac{2(\mathbf{v}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda})}{\lambda_{\text{rf}}} = \frac{2 v_T \cos\theta}{\lambda_{\text{rf}}}$$where $\lambda_{\text{rf}}$ is the radar carrier wavelength. The radar's clutter notch filter rejects any return with $\vert{}\mathbf{v}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda}\vert{} \le v_{\text{notch}}$, which defines the angular notch window around $\theta = 90^\circ$:$$\vert{}v_T \cos\theta\vert{} \le v_{\text{notch}} \implies \theta \in \left[\frac{\pi}{2} - \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{\text{notch}}}{v_T}\right),\; \frac{\pi}{2} + \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{\text{notch}}}{v_T}\right)\right]$$Closed-Loop Notching Control Law:To enter and hold the notch ($\mathbf{v}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda} = 0$), the target's unit velocity vector $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T$ must be steered orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ and rotated at the exact angular rate of the LOS $\dot{\lambda} = \frac{v_T}{r}$ so that $\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = 0$:$$\mathbf{a}_{n,T}(t) = \text{sat}_{a_{n,\max}}\left[\left(-K_{\text{notch}}(\mathbf{v}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda}) + \frac{v_T^2}{r}\right)\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\lambda}\right]$$where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\lambda} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda} - (\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda})\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T}{\Vert{}\boldsymbol{\lambda} - (\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda})\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T\Vert{}}$ is the unit normal vector in the plane of $\mathbf{v}_T$ and $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$. Once locked at $\theta = 90^\circ$, the target's trajectory traces a logarithmic spiral (or circle of radius $r$ around a stationary emitter) with normal acceleration $a_{n,\text{hold}} = \frac{v_T^2}{r}$.9. Turning into the Missile (Lead-Angle & LOS Rate Saturation)Engagement Mathematics:
From the exact planar equations, $v_{cl} = v_M\cos\delta - v_T\cos\theta$ and $\ddot{\lambda} = \frac{2 v_{cl}}{r}\dot{\lambda} + \frac{a_{T,\perp} - a_{M,\perp}}{r}$. When the target turns aggressively into the missile (driving the aspect angle $\theta$ into the head-on hemisphere $\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta \le \pi$ and crossing the LOS line $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$), two mathematical effects compound:   Because $\cos\theta < 0$ for $\theta > 90^\circ$, the target's velocity adds to the closing velocity:$$v_{cl} = v_M\cos\delta + v_T\vert{}\cos\theta\vert{}$$In the LOS rate dynamics $\ddot{\lambda} = \frac{2 v_{cl}}{r}\dot{\lambda} + \frac{a_{T,\perp} - a_{M,\perp}}{r}$, the positive coefficient $\frac{2 v_{cl}}{r}$ acts as an unstable amplifier on $\dot{\lambda}$ as $r \to 0$.Control Law & Saturation Condition:The target commands maximum normal acceleration toward the LOS line to reverse the sign of the missile's lead angle and maximize $\vert{}\dot{\lambda}\vert{}$:$$a_{n,T}(t) = -a_{n,\max}\cdot\text{sgn}\big(\lambda(t) - \psi_T(t)\big)$$
This forces the missile's required PN command $a_c(t) = N v_{cl}\dot{\lambda}(t)$ to scale quadratically with the elevated closing velocity $v_{cl}$. Evasion succeeds when the required missile acceleration exceeds the missile's structural limit $a_{M,\max}$ or when $\vert{}\dot{\lambda}\vert{}$ exceeds the seeker gimbal slew rate limit $\dot{\theta}_{s,\max}$:
   $$N\big(v_M\cos\delta + v_T\vert{}\cos\theta\vert{}\big)\vert{}\dot{\lambda}\vert{} > a_{M,\max} \quad \text{or} \quad \vert{}\dot{\lambda}\vert{} > \dot{\theta}_{s,\max}$$10. Crank ManeuverGeometric Constraint:The target maintains radar track on an adversary or threat along LOS vector $-\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ by flying at the maximum azimuth gimbal limit $\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max}$ (typically $50^\circ\text{ to }70^\circ$) of its onboard sensor while minimizing forward closure:$$\cos^{-1}\big(\hat{\mathbf{t}}_T \cdot (-\boldsymbol{\lambda})\big) = \theta_{\text{gimbal},\max}$$Control Law & Periodic S-Turn Crank:In the horizontal plane, the desired heading angle $\psi_{\text{des}}(t)$ is offset from the threat LOS angle $\lambda_{\text{threat}}(t) = \lambda(t) + \pi$ by $\pm\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max}$:$$\psi_{\text{des}}(t) = \lambda(t) + \pi + \sigma(t)\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max}, \quad \sigma(t) = \text{sgn}\big(\sin(\omega_{\text{crank}}t)\big) \in \{-1, +1\}$$where $\sigma(t)$ switches polarity periodically at frequency $\omega_{\text{crank}}$ to keep the aircraft inside its lateral airspace corridor. The normal acceleration control law is:$$a_{n,T}(t) = \text{sat}_{a_{n,\max}}\Big[v_T\dot{\lambda}(t) + K_\psi v_T\big(\psi_{\text{des}}(t) - \psi_T(t)\big)\Big]$$Closure Rate Reduction:Once established on the crank angle $\theta = \pi - \theta_{\text{gimbal},\max}$, the target's velocity contribution to closing velocity is reduced by the factor $\cos(\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max})$:$$v_{cl} = v_M\cos\delta + v_T\cos(\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max})$$At $\theta_{\text{gimbal},\max} = 65^\circ$, the target's closure contribution drops to $v_T\cos(65^\circ) \approx 0.423 v_T$ (a 57.7% reduction in target closure speed).11. Speed Change Maneuver (Variable Axial Acceleration)Exact Coupled Radial & Lateral Equations:
Standard PN guidance assumes constant closing velocity ($\ddot{r} = 0$, so $a_1(t) = \ddot{r}/r = 0$). Minimum Range or Close-In TurnTurn Radius Disparity Mathematics:For any vehicle moving at speed $v$ with maximum normal acceleration $a_{n,\max}$, the minimum instantaneous radius of curvature $R_{\min}$ and maximum heading rate $\omega_{\max}$ are:$$R_{\min} = \frac{v^2}{a_{n,\max}}, \quad \omega_{\max} = \frac{a_{n,\max}}{v}$$Because $R_{\min}$ scales quadratically with speed ($v^2$) while only inversely with $a_{n,\max}$, a slower target ($v_T \ll v_M$) achieves a much smaller turn radius and higher angular turn rate than a fast missile ($v_M \gg v_T$) even when $a_{n,M,\max} \gg a_{n,T,\max}$:$$\frac{R_{T,\min}}{R_{M,\min}} = \left(\frac{v_T}{v_M}\right)^2\left(\frac{a_{n,M,\max}}{a_{n,T,\max}}\right) \ll 1$$Kinematic No-Escape / Escape Boundary (Isaacs' Homicidal Chauffeur Condition):In missile body-fixed coordinates centered on the missile at $\mathbf{r}_M$ with the $x_B$-axis along $\mathbf{v}_M$, the target's relative position $(x_{\text{rel}}, y_{\text{rel}}) = (r\cos\delta, r\sin\delta)$ cannot be intercepted if it lies inside the missile's left or right minimum turning circles of radius $R_{M,\min}$ centered at $(0, \pm R_{M,\min})$:$$x_{\text{rel}}^2 + \left(|y_{\text{rel}}| - R_{M,\min}\right)^2 < R_{M,\min}^2 \implies r < 2 R_{M,\min}|\sin\delta| = \frac{2 v_M^2}{a_{n,M,\max}}|\sin\delta|$$When the target initiates a maximum-rate close-in turn $\mathbf{a}_{n,T} = a_{n,T,\max}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T$ at range $r \le \frac{2 v_M^2}{a_{n,M,\max}}|\sin\delta|$, the missile is mathematically guaranteed to pass outside the target's trajectory.13.
